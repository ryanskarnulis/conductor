Panel Studies

Five ways to draw the text panel beside the web. All five read the same scripted run as the Living Web page, so pick a moment and compare them side by side.

t + 0.0 s
A

Thread

A conversation. Steps show as chips inside Conductor’s reply, and receipts arrive as attachments.

Good for
Feels familiar at a desk; the order of the talk is obvious.
Costs
Grows long; status is small type inside bubbles.
B

Silk line

One strand from top to bottom. Every step is a knot on it, with its receipt hanging underneath.

Good for
Cause and effect at a glance; echoes the web, and replaces the separate ledger.
Costs
Denser than the others; timestamps add noise.
C

Board

Sorted by what you need to do: needs you, working, up next, done.

Good for
Acting fast; a confirmation is never buried under other steps.
Costs
Loses the order things happened in.
D

Glance

One big sentence about right now, a progress bar, the last receipt. Everything else folds away.

Good for
Room and wall, voice-first use, reading from across the room.
Costs
Detail is one tap away; past steps disappear quickly.
E

Receipts

Each step prints a ticket. Pending ones are outlines; done ones are paper, stamped, with their undo.

Good for
Trust and undo; what changed, where, is the main thing on screen.
Costs
The conversation takes a back seat.

Each panel carries every status as a word, not only a colour. During “Voice only” the screen is off, so every panel dims.
